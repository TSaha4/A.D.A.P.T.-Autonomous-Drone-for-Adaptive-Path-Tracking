\begin{abstract}
Flood relief missions require an operator to identify delivery points, avoid hazards, transform map pixels into coordinates and encode autopilot commands. This paper evaluates \adapt{}, a pipeline that converts flood-annotated map rasters and weather into QGroundControl WPL~110 missions. Operator-sampled HSV segmentation and a terrain-free spread rule produce obstacles; nearest-neighbour ordering and per-leg D*~Lite produce routes. A second mode selects up to $K$ bases and plans battery- and payload-limited sorties with a geometric crossing check. The evaluation uses three demonstration maps without georeferencing and deterministic operator samples from the evaluated implementation. Its independent WPL validator accepts all recorded missions; 98\,789 representable coordinate round trips agree within $6.8\times10^{-8}$~pixels, and tests detect 31 of 33 injected defects. Under moderate weather, increasing $K$ from one to four raises served drops from 10 to 18 of 22 for Varanasi and from 17 to 34 of 41 for Kanpur. However, forecast obstacles cause all 14 single-UAV no-path legs, one thematic map is misclassified as 83\% flood, and workload is unbalanced. The maps lack ground truth and georeferencing, and no ground-station or SITL execution has been recorded. The results establish software-level behaviour for the evaluated system, not geographic accuracy or flight readiness.
\end{abstract}
