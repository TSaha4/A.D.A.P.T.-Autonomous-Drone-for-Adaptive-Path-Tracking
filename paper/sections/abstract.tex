\begin{abstract}
Planning uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) relief flights after a flood still requires an operator to read a flood map, choose delivery points, route around inundated areas and author an autopilot mission by hand. Prior work treats flood mapping, flood modelling, path planning and relief logistics largely separately and seldom produces a mission that an autopilot can load. This paper presents \adapt{}, an open pipeline that converts a flood-annotated map raster and current weather into a QGroundControl WPL~110 mission. It combines operator-sampled HSV segmentation and morphological filtering, a weather-parameterised spread rule that inflates flood obstacles, one boundary drop point per flood region, nearest-neighbour visit ordering, per-leg D*~Lite grid planning and a flat-earth pixel-to-geodetic conversion with explicit validity checks. The unmodified pipeline is evaluated on three published flood maps of Indian districts with a deterministic simulated operator, and its geospatial and mission layers are verified against an independent WGS84 oracle, a specification-based mission validator and mutation testing. All three missions pass validation, 98\,789 randomised coordinate round trips agree within $7\times10^{-8}$~px, and 22 of 24 injected defects are detected. The evaluation also exposes limitations: predicted-flood obstacles reduce the share of the route over predicted inundation from 33.8\% to 17.9\% and from 47.9\% to 11.6\%, but cause all 14 no-path legs; one thematic map is segmented as 83\% flood; and the maps are not georeferenced. No ground-station, simulation or flight validation has been performed, so \adapt{} is a verified research baseline, not a deployable system.
\end{abstract}
