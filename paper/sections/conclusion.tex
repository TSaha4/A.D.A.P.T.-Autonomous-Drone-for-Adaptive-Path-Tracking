\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\adapt{} integrates annotated-map extraction, a weather-driven obstacle proxy, drop selection, routing, pixel-to-coordinate conversion and WPL mission export. The single-UAV and fleet modes generate structurally valid missions on the demonstration maps. Independent geodesic and WPL checks support consistency with the implemented model; an optional GeoTIFF converter provides a raster-derived geographic path, and a mask-fraction guard rejects the observed Assam failure. ArduCopter SITL and MAVProxy accept a full exported mission, while a shorter simulated flight records the first drop action.

The added experiments delimit that result. On 88 labelled Sen1Floods11 test chips, S1 Otsu, a small S1 U-Net and S2 NDWI achieve micro IoU 0.225, 0.606 and 0.772, respectively; the annotation HSV detector is not a raw-image baseline. The flood spread proxy does not beat persistence on two observed EMS transitions. In SITL, intermediate landing resets the mission index and requires an explicit restart; the second servo command does not create a new PWM transition. A balanced assignment lowers the fleet's optimistic longest-route time by up to 22.9\% at unchanged coverage, whereas OR-Tools assignment alone does not improve it. Reachability collapses as the assumed metres per pixel increases, and a 20\% endurance reduction removes reachable drops. The supplied maps lack a usable georeference and the vehicle model is uncalibrated. The paper therefore provides a reproducible software and simulation baseline; field missions require corrected inputs, a safe route policy and physical validation.
