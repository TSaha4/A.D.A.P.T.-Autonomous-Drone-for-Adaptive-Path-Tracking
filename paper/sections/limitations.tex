\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limits}
\emph{Input validity.} The three supplied PNG maps have neither georeferences nor labelled flood truth. Their configured centre and scale do not match the depicted geography (Section~\ref{sec:geovalid}), so exported coordinates cannot be used for field missions. The optional GeoTIFF converter corrects coordinates only when its raster is co-registered to the annotation; it does not create ground truth or verify registration. The Ghana diagnostic uses a hand label to construct the red input and therefore cannot validate HSV detection. The supplied maps' HSV masks extract annotation colours, not water from raw imagery. The separate Sen1Floods11 benchmark tests S1/S2 flood segmentation, but its sensor methods cannot be treated as direct replacements for the annotation detector without a changed input pipeline. The Assam thematic map is an observed failure: 83.1\% of pixels are marked flood; an optional 80\% guard rejects it, but that threshold is uncalibrated. The ten-seed click study represents simulated operators, not measured people.

\emph{Forecast validity.} The spread rule is a terrain-free dilation proxy. It ignores drainage and elevation, and assumes a weather interval that may differ from the source API's accumulation interval \cite{openmeteo_docs}. In the two EMSR504 observed transitions, it equals persistence rather than improving upon it; both transitions belong to one event. Retrospective reanalysis weather is not an operational forecast, and delineation maps have uncertainty. Lower route exposure to the proxy's own predicted area is not flood forecast skill.

\emph{Route and mission validity.} Single-UAV planning samples one cell per $5\times5$ block, can place drops in blocked cells, and flies straight when a leg is disconnected. The 14 observed no-path legs remain unsafe, and the mode has no endurance check. Multi-base drops are dry only under the computed mask, with no landing-site or crowd-safety assessment. The battery coefficients and assumed wind penalty lack flight-log calibration. Balanced assignment improves the longest-route proxy in two cases but increases total route length. A route crossing check provides no separation in time or altitude.

\emph{Study design.} End-to-end results use three convenience graphics and one label-rendered georeferenced diagnostic, while labelled segmentation uses 88 held-out Sen1Floods11 chips. Geospatial chip errors measure a scalar approximation of supplied GeoTIFF transforms, not field position accuracy. The two flood transition pairs come from a single emergency activation. Platform-specific fleet reruns differ by one Kanpur drop (34 versus 35 of 41); the cause is unresolved. Runtimes are single laptop measurements and are not algorithmic scaling estimates. The related-work search was targeted rather than systematic.

\emph{Operational validation.} The full map WPL was accepted in ArduCopter SITL and uploaded/downloaded through MAVProxy, but not flown; a short WPL produced one servo transition, showed intermediate LAND resets the mission index, and reached the second drop only after an explicit restart. With no servo reset, a second release was not demonstrated. No QGroundControl or Mission Planner graphical-interface test, PX4 test, hardware-in-the-loop test or physical flight is available. Before operational use, the system needs georeferenced input, safe disconnected-leg handling, landing-site assessment, a calibrated vehicle and release mechanism, vehicle failsafes and applicable aviation approval. Multiple vehicles also need temporal or vertical deconfliction.
