\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\emph{Input validity.} The three supplied PNG maps have no georeference or labelled flood truth. Their configured centre and scale do not match the depicted geography (Section~\ref{sec:geovalid}), so exported coordinates cannot be used for field missions. Segmentation measures annotation colour, not water in raw imagery. The Assam thematic map is an observed failure: 83.1\% of its pixels are classified as flood. Operator-click and weather variability were not sampled statistically. The colour-margin sweep changes the mask, but does not supply IoU, precision or recall.

\emph{Forecast validity.} The spread rule is a terrain-free obstacle-inflation proxy, with no observed next-frame comparison. It ignores drainage and elevation and interprets current precipitation as an hourly quantity even though the API may report accumulation over a shorter interval \cite{openmeteo_docs}. Lower route exposure to its own predicted area is not evidence of flood forecast skill. No-prediction is the only tested forecast baseline.

\emph{Route and mission validity.} The single-UAV mode uses one sampled cell per $5\times5$ block, can place drops in blocked cells, and flies a straight line when a leg is disconnected. Its 14 observed no-path legs therefore remain unsafe. It has no endurance check. Multi-base drops are dry under the program's mask, but have no independent landing or crowd-safety assessment. Multi-base battery coefficients are uncalibrated; wind is charged as a full headwind on every leg. The nearest-feasible assignment can leave Kanpur's longest mission at 11~km through $K=3$. Crossing repair checks geometry only; it does not provide separation in time or altitude. Intermediate LAND/TAKEOFF reload behaviour is untested. These limitations also explain why standard multi-depot VRP and balancing baselines remain needed.

\emph{Study design.} Results come from three convenience maps, one deterministic simulated operator, one single-UAV weather setting and two fleet weather settings. Runtimes are single measurements on one laptop, sometimes taken alongside parallel runs. The ground scale changes feasibility drastically: at the landmark-estimated Varanasi and Kanpur scales no drop is reachable. Neither flood-spread accuracy nor battery calibration can be inferred from these experiments. The related-work search was targeted rather than systematic.

\emph{Operational validation.} The WPL validator checks format and model invariants, not acceptance or execution by an autopilot. No ground-station load, SITL, telemetry, hardware-in-the-loop or flight results are present in the pushed commit. Before any operational use, the exact missions require georeferenced inputs, coordinate inspection, SITL checks of servo timing, RTL and reload semantics, vehicle-specific failsafes, verified release hardware, a trained pilot and applicable aviation approval. Multiple vehicles additionally require temporal or vertical deconfliction.
